How a coalition is built

Why no party wins alone, how the president picks who forms a government, and what happens in the haggling over portfolios.

Why a coalition is needed

The Knesset has 120 members, and a government needs the support of at least 61 to survive. A single party almost never gets there alone, so parties join a coalition: an agreement in which each party gets something in return for its support of the government.

Who forms the government

After the elections the president consults the parties and gives the mandate to form a government to the member of parliament with the best chance of succeeding, not necessarily the head of the largest party. The one forming the government has 28 days, which can be extended by 14. If they fail, the mandate passes to someone else, and in the extreme case there is a new election.

What is negotiated

Each party demands ministerial portfolios, deputy ministers, committee chairs, budgets for causes that matter to its voters, and certain laws. Sometimes there is also a rotation (two people swap a position) or a veto right on a sensitive issue. The agreement is written down, and whoever breaks it risks bringing the government down.

How it works in the game

In "Government of Ishmael" you go through every stage: you can drop demands, offer incentives, choose who from the party gets each portfolio, and every commitment gets a due date and a reminder. A breach can bring the coalition down.

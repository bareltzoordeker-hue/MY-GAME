How a law is born

From a private bill to the third reading: the stages every law goes through in the Knesset, and why most laws never reach the end.

A proposal and the preliminary reading

A member of parliament can submit a private bill. To advance, it has to pass a preliminary reading in the plenum. A government bill goes straight to a committee, which is why it is easier to advance.

Committee and the first reading

The committee is the room where the law is actually shaped: the clauses are discussed, experts are heard and changes are made. Then the bill returns to the plenum for a first reading, and again to committee for its final wording.

The second and third readings

In the last readings each clause and the law as a whole are put to a vote. An ordinary law passes with a majority of those voting. Amending a Basic Law usually requires a majority of 61 members, and sometimes more.

How it works in the game

In Government of Ishmael real laws go through the same stages over several turns. Each party votes according to its values, and some parties have red lines they will not cross even from inside the coalition. You can push a bill, soften it or give it up.

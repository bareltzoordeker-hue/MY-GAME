Political capital: the currency you cannot buy

What political capital is, how it is gathered, and why it runs out at the least convenient moment.

What political capital is

Political capital is a leader's ability to ask for favors, gather support and pass hard decisions. It is not measured in money. It comes from trust, from successes and from good relations with people who matter.

How it is gathered and spent

Winning an election, a law that passed, a promise that was kept and good relations with partners grow it. Sharp decisions, broken promises and firing ministers wear it down. Someone who spends all of it on one decision will find they have nothing to offer next time.

The important rule

Political capital should be saved for the moment it is truly needed: a crisis, a disputed vote or a big operation. A leader who hands it out at every opportunity discovers they have no strength when it counts.

How it works in the game

In the game every action costs political capital, which renews each turn. Persuading ministers before a big decision costs capital but raises the chance. It is worth checking what an action costs before clicking, with the advisor's explanation.

The security cabinet and who decides on an operation

How a decision on a military operation is made: the prime minister, the defense minister and the cabinet, and what is weighed before going ahead.

Who sits in the room

The political-security cabinet is a committee of ministers chaired by the prime minister. It includes, among others, the defense, foreign and finance ministers. They hear the chief of staff and the heads of the intelligence services, and decide on significant security steps.

What is weighed

Every operation balances several things: the chance of success, the expected damage to the enemy, the risk to the fighters, the harm to civilians on the other side, the enemy response and the international price. Up-to-date intelligence, the stock of munitions and the readiness of the units affect each of them.

The hour and the choice of forces

A strike at night is more of a surprise and harms fewer civilians, but is more dangerous to the fighters. One unit leads and the others cover. An infantry force will not lead an air strike, and a wide ground operation needs combat engineering.

How it works in the game

In the game's planning room you choose a target, forces, an hour and rules of engagement, see the odds and the price, try to persuade ministers in the cabinet, and after the operation get a detailed report with successes, casualties and long-term consequences.

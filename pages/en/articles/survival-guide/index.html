Seven tips to survive as prime minister

A strategy guide for new players: how to keep a stable coalition, a balanced budget and a public that stays off the streets.

Coalition above all

Stay in touch with every party in the coalition, and keep promises on time. Breaking a coalition agreement is the fastest way to lose the government.

Budget and taxes

A high deficit raises interest and debt. Do not cut every ministry at once: a service that deteriorates turns into a crisis. A gradual change in a few areas is better.

Listen to the public

Following the population groups shows who is satisfied and who is angry. Protests begin with a group you neglected for a long time.

Mind security

Fronts that look quiet can flare up. Maintain units, update intelligence and keep stocks. An operation without preparation costs in casualties and in image.

Mind the machinery

Strong ministers want influence, and frustrated ministers threaten to quit. Listen to their requests, and approve what makes sense.

Read the advisor

The advisor runs a simulation before recommending and explains what each action will do. The decision remains yours, but it is worth knowing what to expect.

Play for the long run

Elections come every few years. Decisions that look good next month may cost in the next election. Think about the polls, not just the turn.

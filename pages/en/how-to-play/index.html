How to play

The full guide: roles, time, the campaign, the election, the coalition, legislation, security and diplomacy.

🎯 The goal

Stay in politics and make a difference: get through the election, form a government or lead the opposition, pass the laws and budgets that matter to you and keep the coalition and the public with you.

🧭 Getting started

Choose who to play: any member of the Knesseton or candidate, per the September 2026 lists, or a character of your own who takes a candidate's place on the list (including the place and role). You start in the real person's actual role:

  • Prime minister – budget, taxes, appointments, security and diplomacy, and also the campaign.
  • Minister – one ministry with dedicated actions; extra money and big moves need the prime minister's approval.
  • Party leader – campaign, alliances, coalition negotiations, and in the opposition also no-confidence motions.
  • Knesseton member – committees, speeches, private bills, connections and primaries.

⏭ How time passes

A regular turn is two months. In the four months before the election every turn is a two-week campaign turn. In each turn you make as many decisions as you want and then move on to the next turn. At the end of the turn the economy moves, the polls update, enemies grow stronger and politicians remember what you did. A detailed summary of the turn appears.

🎯 Resources

  • Political capital – the currency of most actions. Renews every turn according to your role and popularity.
  • Popularity and reputation – how much the public likes you and how seriously they take you.
  • Relations – every politician and party remembers: promises, attacks, backing and betrayals.
  • Money – the state budget, and in a party, the campaign fund.

🚩 Campaign and speeches

A party leader opens the campaign: a strategy (security, economy, social justice, religion and state, change or stability), two target audiences, a budget and a slogan. A strategy on an issue the public is burning about pays off more. During the campaign: house meetings, rallies, broadcasts, debates, internal polls, endorsements from public figures, negative campaigning (which can backfire) and a get-out-the-vote drive on election day.

The "🎤 Speech" button is always available: choose a venue, topic, position and tone, and write it yourself or ask for a draft. Every party reacts according to its values, and a sharp speech on a burning issue can trigger a counter-campaign.

🤝 Election and coalition

On election day seats are allocated by the Bader-Ofer method, with an electoral threshold. The president assigns the task of forming a government to whoever got the most recommendations, and there are 28 days (plus a 14-day extension) to reach 61. Every party demands portfolios, deputy ministers, committee chairs, budgets, laws, vetoes and sometimes rotation. You can agree, drop demands or offer additions. The agreement is written, and every commitment gets a deadline and a reminder. A breach angers the partner and can bring the government down. Whoever fails – the mandate passes on, and if no one succeeds, it's back to elections.

📜 Legislation

Dozens of real laws. A private bill starts with a preliminary reading; a government bill goes straight to committee. The process takes several turns. Every party votes by its values; a partner that opposes a government bill is absent from the vote rather than voting against, but red lines are not crossed. Basic laws need 61. When the Knesseton is dissolved there is no legislation.

🛡️ Security and diplomacy

Six fronts – Gaza, Lebanon, Iran, Yemen, Judea and Samaria, and Syria – with a threat level that rises over time. The prime minister and defense minister plan operations with IDF units: from a targeted strike to a broad ground operation. Large operations need the approval of the political-security cabinet, and every operation has a price: casualties, international reaction, reserves and the economy.

Alongside the military track there is a diplomatic one: secret and open channels with the US, Egypt, Qatar, Jordan, the UAE, Saudi Arabia and the Palestinian Authority; mediated ceasefires; confidence-building steps; transfer of territory among Areas A, B and C in Judea and Samaria; and normalization. Every step has political consequences at home.

🕸️ Relations

The relations map shows the relations between the parties and between you and the members of the Knesseton. Meetings, joint events, mutual support in legislation, public defense and help in primaries build ties; attacks wear them down. Party leaders can meet, sign surplus-vote agreements and offer a joint run.

🧠 The advisor

The advisor is available at any moment: he flags the most urgent issue, explains exactly what each button will do, and runs a simulation before recommending in events. The decision is always yours.

🏁 When the game ends

When the party doesn't pass the threshold, when you weren't elected to the Knesseton, when you were ousted, when you were expelled from the party or when you retired. Losing an election doesn't end the game – you continue in the opposition. At the end you get a career summary.

💡 Tips

  • Hovering over a button explains what it does. A "?" next to a number explains where it comes from.
  • Inside the game you can restart the guided tutorial from the "Save and settings" screen.
  • The game saves automatically at the end of every turn, in your browser only. You can export it to a file.
  • No sound? Browsers only play sound after a first click.
